The two series come from different publishers - Econopolis and Gabelli - and are drawn side by side only because no single source covers all five firms. They are not one dataset and should not be read as one. Where they overlap they roughly agree: Sonova at 25% and 24%.

Neither publisher states whether these shares are measured by units shipped or by revenue. Average selling prices in this market differ several-fold between premium prescription devices and OTC ones, so the two bases can name different leaders. This is the single largest unresolved question about the chart.

The named shares do not reconcile with the reported top-five total. Econopolis's three figures alone reach 69%, and adding Demant in the low twenties reaches roughly 91%, which leaves Starkey under about 1.5% of a market it is listed as a top-five member of. The two sources are measuring different things, different years, or both.

Starkey is privately held and no source gives it a share. It is the residual in every table here, so the remainder is not broken out rather than being zero.

The denominator is not settled. Published 2025 totals for this market run from $7.5B to $15.11B, and one publisher reaches $21.61B by counting hearing implants and Cochlear Limited as part of the market. That is a spread of nearly three to one driven by category boundary, not measurement error - so a percentage here means little until you know which market it is a percentage of.

Every published total appears to be measured at retail prices, since retail accounted for 70.6% of the channel. A manufacturer's share computed against a retail-priced total is understated by the whole dispensing margin.

Sonova and Demant both manufacture and own the retail chains that dispense their products, so their group revenue is not comparable with a manufacturer-only competitor's without separating the wholesale line.

The most consequential fact here invalidates the chart rather than adding to it: Amplifon has agreed to acquire GN Hearing for EUR 2.3 billion. GN's roughly 17% would move inside a company that was previously a pure retailer, and every source describing a five-manufacturer market is describing one that is ending.
