This report is one term short of an arithmetic, and it is worth being precise about which. Sizing at wholesale is COUNT x VALUE. The COUNT is settled: EHIMA publishes 23.16 million units for 2025, free, with a six-year history, and defined as exactly the thing this report measures — net wholesale units sold by manufacturers to dispensers. The VALUE is not: no worldwide per-unit wholesale price has been published, and the best figure found is $774, for the United States, in 2019.

Those two are NOT multiplied here, deliberately. 23.16 million worldwide units times a seven-year-old United States average price would produce a confident-looking number describing no market that exists. The terms come from different populations and different years, and the product of two mismatched terms is not an estimate, it is an artefact.

What it would take to close this is one number: a current worldwide average wholesale selling price. That is precisely the term a commissioned study prices, and it is the reason a paid report can state a wholesale total when free sources cannot. Everything else needed is already here.

The published totals that do exist — $7.5B and $9.1B for 2025 — state no price level at all, so neither can be read as a wholesale figure. One publisher reports retail stores as 70.6% of the channel alongside its total, which suggests these are retail-measured.

The distance between the two price levels is not a rounding difference. Dispensers mark up three to four times, so a wholesale total and a retail total for the identical devices differ by roughly that multiple — larger than the entire spread between the published totals, and larger than any error the choice of publisher introduces.

The wholesale price range found, $400 to $3,500 per unit, is nearly an order of magnitude wide. It bounds the value term rather than pricing it, and multiplying the unit count by either end of it gives $9B or $81B.

The evidence would not support a 'trend' chart (series 'EHIMA units sold' has 1 points; trend needs 2), so it is drawn as a 'table'. That is a fact about how much this run established, not a formatting choice.

Published totals for this market disagree by more than half again, from $774 to $400-3500. In every case examined so far a spread this wide came from the category boundary or the price level rather than from measurement error, so the useful question is what each publisher counted, not which is correct. They are not averaged, deliberately: the mean of two definitions describes no market.

No figure here is shown with its arithmetic. Every filing in the corpus states a size as a count times a qualifying rate times a value per unit, with each term sourced, because that is what lets a reader disagree with it. Treat these as quoted totals rather than as a sizing this report performed.
