## SEC filing excerpts — `market-corpus/`

Roughly 725KB of excerpts from registration statements filed with the US
Securities and Exchange Commission. Every file, its filer, its accession number
and a direct EDGAR link are recorded in
[`market-corpus/meta/sources.md`](market-corpus/meta/sources.md), with a
checksum so any file can be verified against what EDGAR serves.

**Why they are here.** The market report schema in
[`market-corpus/SCHEMA.md`](market-corpus/SCHEMA.md) is derived from what these
documents actually contain, rather than from a description of what a market
report ought to contain. Keeping the source material makes that derivation
checkable — the alternative is asking a reader to trust a summary of documents
they cannot see.

**What they are not.** They are not covered by this project's MIT licence, are
not redistributed as part of the installable package (`market-corpus/` is
excluded from the wheel), and are not used at runtime by any code path. They are
inputs to a research process that is finished.

**On availability versus licence.** EDGAR provides free public access to
filings, and that access is what makes this corpus reproducible. Public
availability is not the same as public-domain status. 17 U.S.C. §105 places
works of the US Government in the public domain, and that covers material
prepared by government employees in the course of their duties — it does not
automatically extend to privately authored documents merely hosted by an agency.
A registration statement is written by the filer and its counsel.

The practical position taken here: these are short excerpts of publicly filed
documents, used for research and analysis, attributed precisely, unmodified, and
retained only as long as they are the evidence for a documented conclusion. This
is not legal advice, and anyone redistributing this repository commercially
should form their own view.

**If you would rather not have them.** Delete `market-corpus/sections/` and
`market-corpus/studies/`. Nothing in `deckscope/` or `marketreport/` imports
them, no test reads them, and the schema they produced stands on its own with
the provenance table as its citation trail.
